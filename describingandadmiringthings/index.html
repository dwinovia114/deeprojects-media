A What Is a Descriptive Text?

A descriptive text describes a particular person, animal, place, or thing.

Its purpose is to give detailed information about the subject so that the reader can identify and imagine it clearly.

Common Subjects

People
Mother, Friend
Animals
My Cat, Rabbit
Places
School, Bedroom
Things
Backpack, Bike

Crucial Distinction

A descriptive text focuses on a particular subject, not a general one.

Cats are small animals. They have four legs...

Describes cats in general (Report Text).

My cat, Milo, is a small and playful cat. He has...

Describes one particular cat (Descriptive Text).

B Generic Structure

1

Identification

Introduces the particular subject. (Who/what is being described?)

"My mother is the most important person in my life. She is a teacher..."
2

Description

Gives detailed info (appearance, personality, habits, parts, etc.)

"She has long black hair and a warm smile. She is kind, patient..."

QUESTION TAGS

Asking for Confirmation

What Is a Question Tag?

A question tag is a short question added to the end of a statement.

We use question tags to:

  • ask for confirmation;
  • check whether information is correct; or
  • invite someone to agree with a statement.

Examples

  • Your school is beautiful, isn't it?
  • She likes reading, doesn't she?
  • They are in the library, aren't they?
  • He can swim, can't he?
  • You don't like coffee, do you?

Notice that each sentence consists of a statement followed by a short question.

Statement + Question Tag

The Basic Rules of Question Tags

Rule 1: Positive Statement + Negative Tag

When the statement is positive, the question tag is generally negative.

StatementQuestion Tag
She is kind,isn't she?
They are students,aren't they?
He can swim,can't he?
You like English,don't you?
She speaks English,doesn't she?
They visited the museum,didn't they?
Examples in context:
  • Your classroom is clean, isn't it?
  • The students are friendly, aren't they?
  • Your brother likes reading, doesn't he?
  • They visited the library yesterday, didn't they?

Rule 2: Negative Statement + Positive Tag

When the statement is negative, the question tag is generally positive.

StatementQuestion Tag
She isn't tired,is she?
They aren't busy,are they?
He can't swim,can he?
You don't like tea,do you?
She doesn't play tennis,does she?
They didn't come yesterday,did they?
Examples in context:
  • The library isn't crowded, is it?
  • They aren't at school, are they?
  • He doesn't like reading, does he?
  • You didn't visit the park, did you?

Remember!

StatementTag
PositiveNegative
NegativePositive

Choosing the Correct Auxiliary Verb

The auxiliary verb in the question tag must match the verb pattern in the statement.

1. Sentences with Be

Use am, is, are, was, or were as appropriate.

StatementQuestion Tag
She is beautiful,isn't she?
They are happy,aren't they?
He was late,wasn't he?
You were tired,weren't you?
2. Simple Present Tense

When a sentence uses an ordinary verb in the Simple Present Tense, use do or does.

StatementQuestion Tag
You like English,don't you?
She likes English,doesn't she?
They play football,don't they?
He plays football,doesn't he?
Remember:
I, you, we, they → do/don't
He, she, it → does/doesn't
3. Simple Past Tense

Use did or didn't when the statement uses an ordinary verb in the Simple Past Tense.

StatementQuestion Tag
She visited the museum,didn't she?
They played football,didn't they?
He didn't attend the meeting,did he?
You watched the movie,didn't you?
4. Modal Verbs

Use the same modal verb in the question tag.

StatementQuestion Tag
She can swim,can't she?
He will come,won't he?
They should study,shouldn't they?
You must finish this task,mustn't you?
5. Special Case: I am

The usual question tag for a positive statement beginning with I am is aren't I?

Example: I am late, aren't I?

For a negative statement:

I am not late, am I?

Using Question Tags in Conversations

Question tags are useful when talking about places, people, and everyday experiences.

Giving Compliments & Expressions

Learn how to praise others and express strong emotions using exclamatory sentences and interjections.

What Is a Compliment?

A compliment is an expression used to say something positive about someone or something. We give compliments to:

  • show admiration
  • appreciate someone's effort
  • express approval
  • make someone feel appreciated

Categories & Examples

Complimenting a Person

  • You look beautiful today!
  • You are very kind.
  • You are a talented student.
  • Your presentation was excellent!

Complimenting a Place

  • Your school is beautiful!
  • The garden looks amazing!
  • Your classroom is very clean!

Complimenting an Animal

  • Your cat is so cute!
  • What a beautiful bird!

Complimenting a Thing

  • Your bag looks lovely!
  • Your drawing is wonderful!
  • What a beautiful painting!

How to Respond to Compliments

When someone gives us a compliment, we should respond politely.

Expression Example Response
Thank you. Thank you!
Thank you so much. Thank you so much!
That's very kind of you. That's very kind of you.
I'm glad you like it. I'm glad you like my drawing.
I'm happy to hear that. I'm happy to hear that.

Example Dialogue

Your drawing is beautiful!
B
Thank you! I'm glad you like it.
A
You are very good at drawing.
B
That's very kind of you.

What Is an Exclamatory Sentence?

An exclamatory sentence expresses a strong emotion, such as deep admiration, surprise, or excitement. It always ends with an exclamation mark (!).

Structure Using WHAT

We use "What" to emphasize a noun or a noun phrase.

Pattern 1: Singular Noun
What + a/an + adj + singular noun!
  • What a beautiful dress she is wearing!
  • What an interesting book this is!
Pattern 2: Plural Noun
What + adj + plural noun!
  • What beautiful flowers they bought!
  • What smart students they are!
Pattern 3: Uncountable Noun
What + adj + uncountable noun!
  • What delicious food you cooked!
  • What lovely weather we have today!

Structure Using HOW

We use "How" to emphasize an adjective or an adverb directly.

How + adjective/adverb + Subject + Verb!
  • How beautiful the garden is!
    Emphasizes the adjective "beautiful".
  • How quickly he runs!
    Emphasizes the adverb "quickly".
  • How smart they are!
    Emphasizes the adjective "smart".

Pro Tip

Exclamatory sentences are often used together with regular compliments to show deeper sincerity and stronger emotion.

Comparing WHAT and HOW

WHAT (Focus on Noun)

  • What a fast car! (Emphasizes the noun "car")
  • What a tall building! (Emphasizes the noun "building")
  • What cute puppies! (Emphasizes the noun "puppies")

HOW (Focus on Adjective)

  • How fast the car is! (Emphasizes the adjective "fast")
  • How tall the building is! (Emphasizes the adjective "tall")
  • How cute the puppies are! (Emphasizes the adjective "cute")

Turning Compliments into Exclamatory Sentences

To make your compliments sound stronger and more enthusiastic, you can change a normal statement into an exclamatory sentence.

Normal Statement Using WHAT Using HOW
He is a smart boy. What a smart boy he is! How smart the boy is!
It is a beautiful painting. What a beautiful painting! How beautiful the painting is!
They are friendly students. What friendly students they are! How friendly the students are!

What Is an Interjection?

An interjection is a word or short expression used to communicate a spontaneous feeling or reaction.

Interjections can express:

  • admiration;
  • surprise;
  • happiness;
  • disappointment;
  • pain; or
  • realization.

Examples:

Wow! Oh! Oops! Ouch! Hurray! Aha!

In this lesson, we focus on interjections used to express admiration, delight, and positive surprise.

Common Interjections

Wow!
Admiration or surprise
"Wow! Your school is beautiful!"
Ooh!
Delight or admiration
"Ooh! What a lovely garden!"
Oh!
Surprise or realization
"Oh! Your drawing is amazing!"
Aha!
Discovery or realization
"Aha! Now I understand why you like this place!"
Hurray!
Joy or celebration
"Hurray! We won the competition!"
Oops!
A mistake
"Oops! I dropped my book."
Ouch!
Pain
"Ouch! That hurts!"
Hmm...
Thinking / hesitation
"Hmm... Let me think about it."

Important:

Not all interjections are compliments. For this lesson, students should focus on expressions that naturally introduce or accompany positive reactions.

For example:

  • Wow! Your painting is beautiful.
  • Ooh! What a lovely cat!
  • Oh! Your school has a wonderful garden!

Using Interjections with Compliments

An interjection can appear before a compliment or an exclamatory sentence.

Pattern 1

Interjection + Compliment

Interjection + compliment

Examples:

  • Wow! Your garden is beautiful!
  • Ooh! Your bag looks lovely!
  • Oh! Your drawing is amazing!
Pattern 2

Interjection + Exclamatory Sentence

Interjection + exclamatory sentence

Examples:

  • Wow! What a beautiful garden!
  • Ooh! What lovely flowers!
  • Oh! How amazing your painting is!
Pattern 3

Interjection + Question Tag

Interjections can also be combined with question tags when a speaker reacts to something and seeks confirmation.

Examples:

  • Wow! Your school is beautiful, isn't it?
  • Oh! The library is comfortable, isn't it?

However, remember that these structures have different functions:

  • Interjection: Expresses a reaction.
  • Compliment: Expresses positive appreciation.
  • Exclamatory sentence: Emphasizes a strong feeling.
  • Question tag: Asks for confirmation or invites agreement.

Using Interjections in Context

Dialogue 1 — Complimenting a Garden

Alya: This is our school garden.
Nadia: Wow! What a beautiful garden!
Alya: Thank you! We take care of it every Friday.
Nadia: The flowers are colorful, aren't they?
Alya: Yes, they are.

Dialogue 2 — Complimenting a Drawing

Rina: What do you think of my drawing?
Dina: Wow! Your drawing is amazing!
Rina: Thank you!
Dina: How carefully you drew the details!
Rina: I'm glad you like it.

Dialogue 3 — Complimenting a Pet

Alya: This is my cat, Milo.
Nadia: Ooh! What a cute cat!
Alya: Thank you. He is very friendly, too.
Nadia: Really? He looks adorable!

Dialogue 4 — Admiring a School Library

Rafi: Have you seen our new library?
Dimas: Oh! It looks wonderful!
Rafi: The library is comfortable, isn't it?
Dimas: Yes, it is. I especially like the reading corner.

Choosing Appropriate Interjections

The best interjection depends on the situation and the speaker's emotion. Consider these examples:

Situation Appropriate Expression
Seeing a beautiful garden Wow! What a beautiful garden!
Seeing colorful flowers Ooh! What lovely flowers!
Discovering a new feature in a library Oh! I didn't notice that before!
Realizing why someone likes a place Aha! Now I understand!
Celebrating a friend's achievement Hurray! You did it!

Teaching note:

In natural English, Wow! is especially useful for expressing admiration. Ooh! can express delight, while Oh! often expresses surprise or realization. Context and intonation influence their meanings.

Integrating the Four Language Elements

The purpose of this meeting is not merely to memorize expressions. Students must learn how to combine them naturally in a conversation.

Consider the following dialogues to see how interjections, compliments, exclamatory sentences, and question tags work together in everyday situations.

Model Dialogue 1: Admiring My Mother

Situation: Two friends are talking about their mothers.

Rina: Look at this picture of my mother!
Dina: Wow! What a beautiful woman she is!
Rina: Thank you! She is very kind and patient.
Dina: She is a teacher, isn't she?
Rina: Yes, she is. She teaches English at a junior high school.
Dina: Oh, that's wonderful! She must be a hardworking person, mustn't she?
Rina: Yes, she is. She always helps her students.
Dina: Aww! How lucky you are to have such a caring mother!
Rina: Thank you. I really admire her.

Analysis of the Dialogue

Language Element Example from the Dialogue Function
Interjection Wow! Expressing admiration
Exclamatory sentence What a beautiful woman she is! Expressing admiration
Question tag She is a teacher, isn't she? Confirming information
Interjection Oh, that's wonderful! Expressing a positive reaction
Question tag She must be a hardworking person, mustn't she? Confirming an opinion
Interjection Aww! Expressing affection
Exclamatory sentence How lucky you are to have such a caring mother! Expressing admiration

Grammar correction: In the model dialogue, the correct question tag for She must be a hardworking person is mustn't she?, not isn't she?, because the statement uses the modal verb must.

Model Dialogue 2: Admiring a Friend's Artwork

Situation: A student shows a painting to a friend.

Alya: Look at my painting!
Nadia: Wow! What a beautiful painting it is!
Alya: Thank you! I made it yesterday.
Nadia: You used many colors, didn't you?
Alya: Yes, I did. I wanted to make it look bright.
Nadia: Oh! How creative you are!
Alya: Thank you. I really enjoy painting.
Nadia: Aww! You should join the school art competition.
Alya: That's a great idea!
